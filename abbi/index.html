/ the style

Where twerk comes from.

Style & history · not Abbi's own biography

New Orleans bounce, early 1990s

Twerking traces to the New Orleans bounce music scene of the early 1990s — DJ Jubilee's 1993 track "Do the Jubilee All" is widely credited with the first recorded use of the word. Bounce artists, Big Freedia most of all, kept the style alive in New Orleans clubs for two decades before the rest of the world noticed.

Older roots, longer line

The movement itself — fast hip and glute isolation over a stable base — connects to West and Central African dance traditions carried through the diaspora, echoed in dances like Côte d'Ivoire's mapouka. Bounce didn't invent the motion; it gave it a name and a beat.

2013: the word goes global

One televised performance in August 2013 pushed "twerking" into everyday vocabulary worldwide — and reopened a real conversation about crediting the Black originators of the style, one the dance community is still having.

Why it matters here

Every twerk clip on Twerkhub, Abbi's catalog included, sits downstream of that history. It's the same reason our perreo history piece exists: context turns a clip you're watching into a style you actually understand.
